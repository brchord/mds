# Comeback & Recomp Plan

Oct 13, 2026 · @Max

## Overview

The goal is to get from about 22% body fat to about 15% by mid-April, regain your old strength by late December, and hold that shape through the move to Europe in July. You'll start dating in January, mid-cut and already visibly improving.

Five phases get you there:

1. **Ramp-up** (Oct 12 – Nov 8): rebuild tendon and joint tolerance at light loads.
2. **Rebuild** (Nov 9 – Dec 20): back to your push/pull style, heavy compounds, small calorie deficit. This is where recomposition is fastest, because returning muscle comes back quickly.
3. **Holidays** (Dec 21 – Jan 4): eat at maintenance and train 3–4 days. The aim is to lose nothing, not to gain.
4. **Cut** (Jan 5 – Apr 12): a focused fat-loss phase while training stays heavy.
5. **Maintain and build** (Apr 13 – June): settle at your new body weight, push strength, and prepare a travel-friendly routine for the move.

Fat around the abdomen is usually the last to go, and you can't spot-reduce it with ab work. It comes off with overall fat loss, so expect the midsection to change most in the second half of the cut.

## Phase 1: Ramp-up (Oct 12 – Nov 8)

Train 5 days on an upper/lower rotation: Upper / Lower / Upper / Lower / Upper, flipped the next week so the lower body also gets 3 days. Alternate A and B sessions. Calisthenics stay out until week 3.

| Week | Load (% of old working weight) | Sets × reps | Reps in reserve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (Oct 12) | \~60% | 2–3 × 12–15 | 4 |
| 2 (Oct 19) | \~70% | 3 × 10–12 | 3 |
| 3 (Oct 26) | \~80% | 3 × 8–12 | 2–3 |
| 4 (Nov 2) | \~85% | 3 × 8–10 | 2 |

Increase the big compounds by no more than 5–10% a week, even when they feel easy. Machines and cables can climb faster.

**How to progress (double progression):** pick a weight where you reach the bottom of the rep range with your target reps in reserve. Add reps each session until you hit the top of the range on every set, then add weight and start over at the bottom. During the ramp-up, add reps before weight; extra reps are easier on tendons than extra load.

Everything is done as **straight sets**. The one superset is cable curl + triceps pushdown, done on the same cable by swapping the handle.

## Phase 2: Rebuild (Nov 9 – Dec 20)

Run a 5-day split: Push / Pull / Legs / rest / Upper / Lower / rest. Heavy compounds come first, then machines and cables as accessories, the way you used to train. Reps in reserve: 1–2 on compounds and 0–1 on the last set of isolation work.

Use **double progression**. Once you hit the top of the rep range on every set, add weight (2.5–5 kg on compounds) and start again from the bottom of the range.

Your dumbbells top out at 50 lb. When a dumbbell press gets easy at 50 lb for 12 reps, switch it to the barbell version.

**Rep ranges:** stay in the lower half on compounds, where strength comes from, and the upper half on cables and isolation work. During the cut, protect the weight on compounds first; heavy load is what tells your body to keep muscle while eating less.

Every 6–8 weeks, take a **deload week**: same exercises, half the sets, about 10% less weight. The holiday break counts as one.

## Phase 3: Cut (Jan 5 – Apr 12) and Phase 4: Maintain (Apr 13 – June)

**Cut.** Eat about 400–500 kcal below maintenance and aim to lose 0.5–1% of body weight per week. Over 14 weeks, that's roughly 7–9% of your body weight, which takes most people from about 22% body fat to about 15%.

- Keep the Phase 2 split and keep the weights heavy. Heavy training is what tells your body to hold on to muscle while you eat less.
- If recovery suffers, drop one set from accessories first, never from the main lifts.
- Expect strength to stall or dip slightly late in the cut. That's normal; small losses are fine.
- Around late February, take one week at maintenance calories if you feel flat or hungry all the time. It makes the second half of the cut easier.
- If weight loss stalls for 2 weeks, cut about 150 kcal more or add 2,000 daily steps, not both.

**Maintain (Apr 13 – June).** Raise calories back to maintenance over 2 weeks. Keep the 5-day split and chase strength PRs. If you want to look fuller, eat slightly above maintenance (+150–200 kcal).

In June, build a short **travel routine** for the move: dumbbells, bands, and bodyweight, about 45 minutes, 3–4 days a week. It keeps you training through the weeks of packing, flights, and finding a new gym.

## Nutrition

Your starting point is about 80 kg (175–180 lb) at 1.75 m. At \~22% body fat, that's roughly 62–63 kg of lean mass, so reaching \~15% while keeping your muscle puts you around **73–75 kg (161–165 lb)** by April.

Estimated maintenance is **\~2,600 kcal a day**, based on age 41, lifting 5 days a week and walking about 3 km daily. It's an estimate; confirm it by logging food for 2 weeks and watching your weekly average weight.

At 41, recovery is the limiting factor more than effort. Protect sleep (7–9 hours), warm up thoroughly before heavy sets, and take deloads on schedule rather than waiting until you feel beaten up.

### Your biggest lever: the daily sweets

A sweet cold mocha (roughly 250–400 kcal) plus 2–3 cookies (roughly 150–250 kcal) adds up to about 400–650 kcal a day. That's more than your entire planned deficit during the cut, before counting cereal bars or a Kinder Bueno (about 120 kcal per bar). Calorie figures here are approximate.

You don't need to cut sweets to zero. Give yourself a budget of about 200 kcal of treats a day:

- **Mocha:** switch to iced coffee with milk and a sugar-free syrup, or order the smallest size made with skim milk.
- **Cookies:** one with your coffee, not three.
- **Snack swaps:** Greek yogurt, panela cheese, carne seca, fruit, jícama or cucumber with lime and chile, boiled eggs, or a protein bar instead of a cereal bar. Keep these on hand so the easy option is the good one.

### Office lunch (3× a week)

This is a good meal: lean protein plus salad. Since it's your one big meal there, keep the large portion but make it mostly protein and vegetables: a double serving of meat or chicken, salad, and one normal serving of rice, potatoes, or tortillas. Broth soups are fine; skip cream soups during the cut. On office days, a protein-heavy breakfast at home (eggs or Greek yogurt) stops the afternoon snack cravings.

### Eating out (4×+ a week)

- **Tacos al pastor:** choose corn-tortilla tacos over gringas. The flour tortilla and cheese roughly double the calories per piece. Decide your number before ordering (4–5 in the rebuild, 3–4 during the cut).
- **Italian:** choose a protein main or a tomato-based pasta over cream sauces. Have one piece of bread or skip the basket, and share dessert.
- **McDonald's (1× a week):** keep it, but plan it: one burger, small fries, zero-sugar drink. Skip large combos and shakes.
- **During the cut:** aim for 2–3 meals out a week instead of 4+.

### At home

Instant broth soups are low in calories but nearly protein-free. Add shredded chicken or two eggs to make them a real meal. Quesadillas can come back too: corn tortillas, a moderate amount of panela or Oaxaca cheese, and chicken inside.

### Hitting 160 g of protein

A rough day: breakfast 30–40 g (3 eggs + yogurt), lunch 50–60 g, snack 20–30 g, dinner 40 g. If you're often short, one whey shake (about 25 g) fills the gap.

### Other habits

- Alcohol is a hidden obstacle when dating. During the cut, keep it to 1–2 nights a week and choose lower-calorie drinks.
- Creatine monohydrate, 3–5 g a day, is the one supplement worth taking. It's cheap and well studied.

## Cardio

Your 3 km daily walk is already about 4,000 steps. Aim for **8,000–10,000 steps a day** in total; it burns meaningful calories without hurting recovery.

- **Phase 1:** walking only, so your legs adapt to lifting first.
- **Phase 2 onward:** 2 sessions a week of 30–45 minutes of easy cycling or swimming, at a pace where you could still talk. Put them on rest days or after upper-body days, not right before leg days.
- **Cut:** optionally add a third session, or make one session intervals (for example, on the bike, 6–8 rounds of 30 seconds hard and 90 seconds easy). Keep it to one hard session a week so leg training doesn't suffer.
- **Your pool:** about 20 m is fine for continuous easy swimming or intervals. Track time rather than distance.

Running isn't necessary. Cycling and swimming are easier on your joints, and you already have years of experience with them.

## Tracking, recovery and warning signs

With recomposition, the scale can barely move while you change a lot, so track more than weight.

| What | How often | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Body weight | Daily, judge the weekly average | Day-to-day swings are mostly water |
| Waist at the navel | Weekly, same morning | Best signal for abdominal fat loss |
| Photos (front, side, back) | Every 4 weeks, same light | Shows changes you won't notice in the mirror |
| Main lifts (weight × reps) | Every session | Confirms you're building, not losing, muscle |

A shrinking waist with steady or rising lifts means it's working, even if the scale is flat.

**Recovery:** sleep 7–9 hours. It affects fat loss and muscle retention as much as diet does.

**Back off when you notice:**

- Joint or tendon pain, especially in the elbows, shoulders, or knees, as opposed to muscle soreness
- Soreness that lasts more than about 3 days
- Lifts dropping for 2 or more sessions in a row
- Poor sleep, constant fatigue, or no motivation for over a week

First response: take a deload week. If pain in a joint persists after that, see a physiotherapist before pushing through.
